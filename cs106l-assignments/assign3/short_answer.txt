Q1. What’s const-correctness and why is it important?
Const-correctness means using the const keyword properly to indicate which variables, function parameters, or member functions should not modify the data they refer to.
It's about promising not to change something that shouldn't be changed --- and letting the compiler help you enforce that promise.
Why const-correctness is important:
* Prevents accidental modification
** The compiler stops you from changing things that shouldn't change.
* Makes code easier to understand
** When const is seen, you would immediately know: "This function won't modify the object".
* Enables const objects and references
* Improves compiler optimizations
** The compiler can make more assumptions about immutability.
Q2. Is your class const-correct? How do you know?
My class is already const-correct in the sense that every "read-only" operation is marked const,
and all mutating operations are non-const. I can call all getters and display() on a const ArmID, but I can't call the setters --- exactly what we want.
